# Week 1: Starting At VFMC (Victorian Funds Management Corporation)
Walking in on day one at VFMC was fairly nerve racking. Being my first office job, I did not really know what to expect. VFMC is an investment firm for Victorian Government bodies. I was assigned to work with the risk team, which consists of ten people; of them, I worked most closely with six. My induction covered what the organisation does and its goals, after which I was shown the open plan floor and where I would be sitting. 2 the main analysts that I would work with were Ben, who manages the team, and James, a senior analyst who was my main source of help.

After all of this, I was assigned my task, which was to develop a manager concentration dashboard that tracks how much money sits with each investment manager. Before this, this job would have been done by hand, but with around $100 billion in investments being managed, nobody would be able to eyeball that reliably and efficiently. The value of my role is to aggregate the scattered data and create something that flags when a manager has too much invested in them, so the business can act before it becomes a problem. 

The importance of this role was shown to me early on, as in the first meeting I attended, the team was investigating the investment firm Situational Awareness LP, which lost 67% of its fund. The copy seemed strong on the surface but showed some qualitative problems, such as having a small team of 8. Qualitative analysis is one of the things that my dashboard must also track. This showed me the importance of risk analysis and how the dashboard will assist in decision-making. As James puts it, “we work in the business of taking risk,” which tells me that accuracy and timing are important when creating the dashboard, as the team aims to take controlled risk.

With an understanding of the goals and the problem that needed to be fixed.

Overall, the biggest surprise to me was the work culture. Having a relaxed schedule and deadlines, with a lot of collaborative work, I was surprised by how everyone is trusted to get things done. 

With my outlook on the task, I am confident that I can achieve this, aware that I do not want to let the team down.
