# Week 2: Planning and prototyping. While understanding how AI is used in the workplace

My second week was for planning. I created the plan and laid out the requirements with James. This is where we went back and forth, showing him drafts and asking questions, like what he would like a certain feature to do or how he wanted something to be displayed. Coming in without a financial background, having lots of questions matters, as I would not be able to create a tool if I did not understand.

Meeting and planning with James made the purpose of the dashboard clear. The purpose is to check the limit of the amount of investment in each manager. This is comparable to having too many eggs in one basket. If too many sit with one manager and the measure runs into trouble, the exposure is disastrous. VFMC has internal rules governing this

The most interesting part of this week was how we built the front end. James is a strong advocate for AI and suggested using Copilot to generate an HTML mock-up, which would be used as the front end later on. As I have never built or designed a dashboard, this method made it easy to use and visualise; having this front end created within the week gave me much relief, leaving me to focus purely on the backend.

What surprised me the most was not only the speed it took to create such a product, but the fact that this could take weeks to produce something as useful, yet was nearly completed in a single day. All it took was a few more iterations and prompting about the requirements of the small details, and we had a working front end, filled with dummy data.
 
James and Ben described to me the old process they had to do before creating such a product. They said it would take days of meetings with designers and programmers before anything was built. AI compresses that down, but it still proves to me that you need to know what you’re asking for and recognise when the output is wrong. 

I also realise the team shared the same opinion on AI as James, seeing it as a positive tool. Most come from a math or financial background rather than a coding one. For my own work, the lesson is that I need fluency with these more than I need to hand-build everything. This has changed my perspective on how work should be done and built my trust in using AI tools.
