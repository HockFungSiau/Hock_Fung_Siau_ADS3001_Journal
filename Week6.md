# Week 6: The questions I kept asking myself

I’m at the halfway mark of my internship, at the start of the month, the team’s busiest stretch. Many of the people I sit with were on leave or busy, which left me time to focus and reflect. My week became a set of questions.

## Is what I am doing correct?

With nobody to check my work and James busy, I doubted whether I was meeting the specifications. I spent most of the week comparing my totals and code logic against the team’s previous work. When known figures were available, I was able to find my mistakes. Overall, while this was a great learning opportunity, I was worried that I wouldn’t deliver a product that was good enough. Comparing my results against their figures helped me measure the quality of my work and address that concern.

## Do I ask Copilot, or wait for a person?

Copilot makes getting information easy, but it makes mistakes and doesn’t include details specific to VFMC. Copilot earlier gave me a good front-end wireframe, but relying on it too much risks errors in areas it doesn’t know. So, this week I felt AI is good for general answers, and anything specific to VFMC goes to a person.

## Do I ask James, or work it out?

I want to show independence, so I don’t ask James about everything, but I also want to hand over a proper product, so I try first, then ask whether there is a better way. That brings back my earlier worry about asking too many questions, but I now think the number matters less than the confidence to ask. Co-workers sometimes admit they’re about to ask a stupid question, and James and others respond saying there is no such thing.

## Can I build something I am not good at?

The front end isn’t my strength, as I’ve never built anything like it. I’ve researched online and used AI to write code that fills the gaps, and I’m pleased with the result. I expected to learn it, but I’m mostly reviewing code rather than writing it. I still need to develop good judgment, but it isn’t the same as building it myself. Currently, my backend is about 60% finished, while the front-end design is done.
