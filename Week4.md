# Week 4: How bad assumptions and lack of knowledge slowed my progress 

I assumed the hard part of this project would be cleaning the data. This assumption was false. I had this assumption because I had always believed that in university outlines, invalid rows and other factors would need to be dealt with before starting. But in VFMC, the data was very well maintained (which I understand would not always be the case). Their data was well structured with lots of detail. What I truly struggled with was understanding each row. By the end of this week of exploration of the data, it had subverted some of the expectations of my data analysis:

## I assumed clean data would be easy to query.

Even with the well organised values, there was far more information that I needed. Pulling exactly what I needed and wanted means that I must know what exists, and I am conscious that there may be things I have missed simply because I do not know what to look for. It took many meetings and messages to the data vault team to get the necessary information that I needed.

## I assumed my total not adding up was a formatting fault

I had left out columns needed to group records correctly. The data was fine, but my understanding of it was completely wrong, a common thing that has happened to me this week. Some rows had the same manager appearing related to different values, caused by improper grouping on my part. Untangling meant testing the total against figures I could verify, which is how I eventually learnt their system 

## I assumed blank parent managers were my mistakes

In my dashboard, I had to create a table that mapped all managers that relate to each other. I thought that there were so many missing values that I had a query error or a merging error. But after consulting with Mimi, I found that the information simply wasn’t filled in

## In the end
This week was a long week, but I ended up finishing the analytical side of the work. I was able to understand the meaning of every column, value and number, and was able to link the formulas for the task. The only thing left was to come up with an idea on how to finish the mapping table.

Overall, I found this week a little frustrating and slow. I believed and assumed that the challenge that I would usually face in a data science cleaning stage was that the data had common formatting errors, but it turned out that I just did not have a strong grasp of the financial knowledge. This has shown me that even though I have a good understand in data quality, it means nothing without a strong understanding of the field you work in. A data scientist has to be flexible and a fast learner to be able to apply their skills. For next week, I plan on gaining better knowledge and understanding of the field, so I do not make assumptions and deal with the task with a better understanding.
