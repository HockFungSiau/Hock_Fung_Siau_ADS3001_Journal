# Week 11: The Dashboard Goes Live

## *A newsletter from the risk desk*

## The first live test
While the team worked through last month’s calculations, James used the current dashboard to check something on a potential manager that would be added. They agreed the figures were fairly similar to what they had calculated, which felt good, because it meant the dashboard was on the right track. Until now I had only tested the dashboard on previous calculations, and those covered just the parts needing attention (Week 9). This time the people who do this every month ran it through their routine, which is stronger evidence than anything I could produce alone. I also got to watch how it worked in other hands. As I don’t know exactly what the next steps are after I leave, it was good to see that they can understand and use the dashboard.

## Close call
I had been worried that the missing data would leave the project unfinished. It didn’t, because the manager James picked was fairly big, so there was data for it. That was quite lucky, but it also showed me the dashboard works: where data exists, it gave back the correct results. It is not yet proven everywhere, because the rest of the data is still missing.

## Data desk
During the week I found the team who can fill the gaps in the data, but the data team cannot do it themselves. They first have to get someone from development to fill in the missing parts, and only then can they fill in the rest. They also mentioned this may be a long wait, which would run past my internship end date. In Week 8 I couldn’t tell who to look for; this week I could. At least in the end I found a way forward on the data problem, by linking James directly with that team, which gives me confidence the project can be finished off easily after I leave VFMC.

## Handover notice
As the coding and product side was done, all that was left was testing on the new data once the data team fills it in. At the end of the week James said he and others could finish the project for me, and we agreed there was no time for me to test it myself. This left me to write handover documents so he and the team can finish the project after I leave, while letting them know when the data would be filled.

## Editor’s note
In Week 6 I was scared of not being able to finish a working dashboard, and in Week 10 I asked whether the team can trust it. Watching it work on live data this week answered that better than my own checking, and it let me feel acceptance, not disappointment, about the unfinished testing. It also changed what “done” means: for me, a tool someone else can pick up is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.
